Warrior DPS Pre-Raid Best in Slot (BiS) - TBC Classic
By Beanna
Updated: 2026/03/02
In this Pre-Raid Best-in-Slot Gear List for Warrior DPS in The Burning Crusade Classic, we will list the recommended gear for Warrior DPS to aim for when gearing up to raids in The Burning Crusade Classic, including gear sourced from Normal and Heroic Dungeons, professions, BoE World Drops, and reputations.


Warrior DPS Best in Slot Hub
Pre-Raid Best In Slot Gear for Warrior DPS in Phase 1
The best pre-raid gear for Arms and Fury Warriors is very similar, because there aren't many options and both primarily focus on reaching the Hit Cap and a high Critical Strike score to help your rage generation and secure a good Flurry uptime.
Since Karazhan is a 10-man raid, your group composition may not always have a Balance Druid Druid or  Draenei to provide additional hit. For this reason, it is recommended to carry a variety of items to adjust your hit on the fly between 5%, 6%, and 9%.

The main difference with gearing in Vanilla is the amount of crafted gear you will want to farm and/or purchase as early as possible. Both the Ragesteel and Fel Leather sets provide excellent stats without breaking the bank and will immediately kickstart your damage output at lvl 70.
Your best weapons, Dragonmaw Dragonmaw for Fury and Lionheart Champion Lionheart Champion for Arms, both require Blacksmithing and will be a serious gold investment when you hit lvl 70. These fantastic weapons will later be upgraded in Phase 2 and remain your best weapons all the way to Phase 3, so they are well worth their initial cost.

Should DPS Warriors Choose Aldor or Scryer for BiS in TBC Classic Phase 1?
Both of the Shattrah factions offer similar shoulders enchants, with a marginal advantage for the Scryers one, providing more Critical Strike rating, more valuable than Attack Power.

    The Aldor can give you access to Vindicator's Brand Vindicator's Brand, a powerful pre-raid off-hand weapon, provided you have the finances to boost this reputation to Exalted early on. On the flip side, the Aldor is also generally the most popular faction, which usually drives the prices for Mark of Sargeras Mark of Sargeras and Fel Armament Fel Armament up compared to the Scryers equivalents.
    The Scryers' advantage primarily resides in its unique Blacksmithing plans, which will help you level Blacksmithing up to lvl 375 to craft your best weapons at a much lower cost. Sunfury Signet Sunfury Signet and Arcane Tome Arcane Tome are also usually cheaper than the Aldor equivalents, both contributing to saving you a decent amount of gold at the start of the expansion.

In conclusion, neither faction will provide a real advantage after the beginning of Phase 1, and it's mostly a choice between an immediate power increase in the form of Vindicator's Brand Vindicator's Brand at a high cost, or saving gold to increase your reputation and level up Blacksmithing.

If you need to grind reputation with The Aldor or The Scryers, or any of the other factions that offer BiS gear for DPS Warriors, take a look at our guides to learn the fastest way to reach the rank you'll need.

Pre Raid Best in Slot Head Armor for DPS Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 1
Mask of the Deceiver is the best pre-raid helmet, providing excellent stats as well as an essential meta socket.
However, because it requires 50 Badges of Justice, you may not be able to purchase it right away. In the meantime, Helm of Assassination, or the quest reward Helm of the Claw, are close alternatives that will let you invest your early Badges of Justice on higher priority items.

If you can't afford a Relentless Earthstorm Diamond Relentless Earthstorm Diamond at the beginning of the expansion because of supply and demand on metagems, then your best pre-raid helmet without a meta socket is the Overlord's Helmet of Second Sight, obtained after a long quest chain.

Pre-Raid Best in Slot Trinkets for DPS Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 1
Badge of the Swarmguard from Ahn'Qiraj is by far the best trinket available outside of TBC raids, and will remain the best trinket for short encounters up to a minute and/or cleave all the way until Phase 4 of TBC. Armor Penetration is always the best stat for physical classes because your targets take exponential damage the lower their armor.

Bosses in TBC have a lot more armor than in Vanilla, and armor reduction effects such as Sunder Armor, Expose Armor and Faerie Fire remove only about half of it, leaving plenty of armor to strip with Badge of the Swarmguard and Armor Penetration when it starts appearing as a stat on items later into the expansion.

Furthermore, Blizzard announced that all raids will be released in their nerfed state for Anniversary TBC, which means lower bosses' health pool and even shorter encounters, making Badge of the Swarmguard all the more relevant.

If you didn't acquire Badge of the Swarmguard from Ahn'Qiraj during Vanilla, you will be at a disadvantage throughout the majority of TBC and should start begging your guild leadership to raid Ahn'Qiraj again for it.

The second-best trinket you can get your hands on outside of raids is the Bloodlust Brooch, available for 41  Badges of Justice and your most important purchase for your first few badges. While it doesn't have nearly the same burst potential as Badge of the Swarmguard, it's a more steady damage increase that will remain good in all situations throughout Phase 1.
Slayer's Crest is basically Bloodlust Brooch with 10% less stats but still a strong third choice, closely followed by Abacus of Violent Odds with similar stats but a slightly less valuable haste on-use.

If an encounter is so short that you may not be able to use two on-use trinkets back to back, then Hourglass of the Unraveller is a valid second trinket, as its proc has a chance to overlap with your on-use effect for exponential returns.

Pre-Raid Best in Slot Weapons for DPS Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 1
Dual-Wield Weapons
The Blacksmithing weapon Dragonmaw is by far the most powerful main-hand weapon for a Fury Warrior in Phase 1. Thanks to its incredible proc, this weapon and the follow-up upgrade in Phase 2 will last you until you acquire either Warglaives or Sunwell weapons in Phase 4! However, this weapon is quite expensive to craft and will require a major gold investment to level up Blacksmithing, craft Drakefist Hammer, and upgrade it to Dragonmaw as soon as possible.

For your off-hand weapon, there are many options to pick from, depending on how much gold you are comfortable spending just for pre-raid gear that will eventually be replaced by Karazhan drops.

If gold is no object, the best off-hand for Fury (and best main-hand for dual-wielding Arms Warriors) is technically Blinkstrike. However, this BoE world drop weapon usually sells for an obscene amount of gold that is hard to justify for an off-hand weapon that will underutilize its proc chance and only be a 1% upgrade. Your next best off-hand is Fel Edged Battleaxe, which is also an expensive weapon to craft for a temporary 0.5% damage increase. While most races will probably skip this weapon, it is worth noting that the axe remains competitive with Karazhan weapons for Orc Orcs only thanks to their Axe Specialization racial.

The next option is Vindicator's Brand, only available once Exalted with the Aldor. Pushing your Aldor reputation to Exalted during the first couple of weeks of the expansion isn't exactly cheap either, so this weapon could also join the "too expensive" category alongside the BoE weapons above, considering it's a marginal DPS increase over heroic weapons.

Most ilvl 115 blue weapons from heroics perform within a couple of DPS of each other, so you have plenty of options there. Most notably, Latro's Shifting Sword, Bloodskull Destroyer and Edge of the Cosmos are the best for Human Human, whereas Firebrand Battleaxe and The Harvester of Souls are the best for Orc Orc Warriors thanks to their respective racials.
Finally, the blue PvP weapons Grand Marshal's Slicer or High Warlord's Cleaver are only slightly worse than heroics weapons, but guaranteed with a bit of grinding in battlegrounds if that's your preference.

    Slow vs Fast Off-Hand

    Starting from TBC, Whirlwind now hits with both of your weapons when dual-wielding, which incentivizes Fury Warriors to use a slow off-hand weapon for higher single-target and AoE damage. That being said, fast off-hand weapons remain fairly close for single-target DPS and provide some valuable quality-of-life advantages, mainly thanks to a steadier rage generation that ensures an easier rotation.

    The main downside of using two slow weapons is the risk of syncing your swings and losing the essential benefits of the Heroic Strike queue feature. This is especially common in Phases 1 and 2, where 2.7 speed raid weapons will match your Dragonmaw speed, and require special attention to your swing timers to ensure your weapons stay out of sync, and manually desync with a macro when needed.

Two-Hand Weapons
As a Two-handed Arms Warrior throughout TBC, you will only want to consider two-handed Swords due to the Sword Specialization talent overshadowing other weapon types.
Your best pre-raid weapon is Lionheart Champion, also an expensive Blacksmithing craft that will require leveling up your profession to lvl 375 and buying a lot of materials. This sword and the follow-up upgrade in Phase 2 will last you until Phase 3, and is on par with the best sword from raids in Phase 2.

The next best sword is the rare Quantum Blade, with a miserable 3% drop chance, and still 5% worse than the Blacksmithing sword. Followed by Endbringer, 9% worse.

    Some players may be tempted to craft the Deep Thunder mace instead for PvP, but be aware that Mace Specialization provides basically zero benefits to your damage in PvE, and you will lose ~7% of your damage by skipping Lionheart Champion. While that may not sound like much to some, 7% damage is the amount of DPS you gain from going from Phase 1 BiS to Phase 2 BiS. So you are essentially cancelling out an entire phase worth of raid upgrades if you show up to raid with a mace instead of a sword.

Arms Warrior DPS Phase 1 Best in Slot (BiS) - TBC Classic
By Beanna
Updated: 2026/05/02
This guide is Wowhead's Best in Slot Gear list from Karazhan, Gruul's Lair, and Magtheridon's Lair for $class $role in The Burning Crusade Classic. This guide will list the recommended gear for $class $role to acquire while they progress on the first few raids in The Burning Crusade Classic, and contains gear sourced from Karazhan, Gruul's Lair, Magtheridon's Lair, heroic dungeons, early PvP grinding, professions, BoE World Drops, and reputations.

We have created many different best-in-slot lists, including a pre-raid list, containing gear you can obtain without doing any raid content.


DPS Warrior Best in Slot Guides
Best In Slot Gear for Arms Warrior DPS in Phase 1
Since Karazhan is a 10-man raid, your group composition may not always have a Balance Druid Druid or  Draenei to provide additional hit. For this reason, it is recommended to carry a variety of items to adjust your hit on the fly between 5%, 6%, and 9%.

Additionally, Arms Warriors looking to add Enchants and Gems to their BiS gear should take a look at our guide below:

Arms Warrior Gems & Enchants
Should Arms Warrior DPS Choose Aldor or Scryer for BiS in TBC Classic Phase 1?
Both of the Shattrah factions offer similar shoulders enchants, with a marginal advantage for the Scryers one, providing more Critical Strike rating, more valuable than Attack Power.

    The Aldor can give you access to Vindicator's Brand Vindicator's Brand, a powerful pre-raid main-hand weapon, provided you have the finances to boost this reputation to Exalted early on. On the flip side, the Aldor is also generally the most popular faction, which usually drives the prices for Mark of Sargeras Mark of Sargeras and Fel Armament Fel Armament up compared to the Scryers equivalents.
    The Scryers' advantage primarily resides in its unique Blacksmithing plans, which will help you level Blacksmithing up to lvl 375 to craft your best weapons at a much lower cost. Sunfury Signet Sunfury Signet and Arcane Tome Arcane Tome are also usually cheaper than the Aldor equivalents, both contributing to saving you a decent amount of gold at the start of the expansion.

In conclusion, neither faction will provide a real advantage after the beginning of Phase 1, and it's mostly a choice between an immediate power increase in the form of Vindicator's Brand Vindicator's Brand at a high cost, or saving gold to increase your reputation and level up Blacksmithing.

If you need to grind reputation with The Aldor or The Scryers, or any of the other factions that offer BiS gear for Arms Warriors, take a look at our guides to learn the fastest way to reach the rank you'll need.

Best in Slot Trinkets for Arms Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 1
Dragonspine Trophy (DST) is the best trinket in the game and, despite only being a Phase 1 drop, will remain the best trinket up until Wrath of the Lich King for Arms Warriors. Due to the rage mechanic, Warrior naturally scales amazingly with haste for more attacks, leading to more rage, leading to more abilities, and exponential gains throughout the expansion. Warrior objectively benefits the most from DST when looking only at hard, cold DPS numbers, both in absolute and relative increases, compared to any other class and specialization.

Badge of the Swarmguard from Ahn'Qiraj is the best trinket for short encounters up to a minute and/or cleave all the way until Phase 4 of TBC.
Armor Penetration is always the best stat for physical classes because your targets take exponential damage the lower their armor. Bosses in TBC have a lot more armor than in Vanilla, and armor reduction effects such as Sunder Armor, Expose Armor and Faerie Fire remove only about half of it, leaving plenty of armor to strip with Badge of the Swarmguard and Armor Penetration when it starts appearing as a stat on items later into the expansion.
Furthermore, Blizzard announced that all raids will be released in their nerfed state for Anniversary TBC, which means lower bosses' health pool and even shorter encounters, making Badge of the Swarmguard all the more relevant.

Bloodlust Brooch is the third-best trinket in Phase 1 and is best suited for longer encounters, thanks to better passive stats while on cooldown. It catches up to Badge of the Swarmguard at around 3 minutes, but not many encounters may last that long in the nerfed raids. Nonetheless, it's a good alternative trinket to rotate on trash mobs while the Badge is on cooldown.

Best in Slot Two-Handed Weapons for Arms Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 1
As a Two-handed Arms Warrior throughout TBC, you will only want to consider two-handed Swords due to the Sword Specialization talent overshadowing other weapon types. While Orc Orcs can lower the gap with Poleaxe Specialization thanks to their Axe Specialization racial, it's still not enough to beat the amazing Sword Specialization talent.

The Blacksmithing weapon Lionheart Champion is by far the most powerful two-hander for an Arms Warrior in Phase 1. Thanks to its incredible proc, this weapon and the follow-up upgrade in Phase 2 will remain competitive until Phase 3. However, this weapon is quite expensive to craft and will require a major gold investment to level up Blacksmithing, craft Lionheart Blade, and upgrade it to Lionheart Champion as soon as possible.

The next best sword is the Gladiator's Greatsword, performing 2% worse than the Blacksmithing sword, followed by Despair, 3% worse and only slightly better than the non-upgraded Lionheart Blade. Gorehowl is on par with Gladiator's Greatsword for Orc Orcs only.

    Some players may be tempted to craft the Deep Thunder mace instead for PvP, but be aware that Mace Specialization provides basically zero benefits to your damage in PvE, and you will lose ~7% of your damage by skipping Lionheart Champion. While that may not sound like much to some, 7% damage is the amount of DPS you gain from going from Phase 1 BiS to Phase 2 BiS. So you are essentially cancelling out an entire phase worth of raid upgrades if you show up to raid with a mace instead of a sword.

Best in Slot Main Hand Weapons for Arms Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 1
Arms Warrior can also be played as an alternative Dual-Wield build — often nicknamed "Kebab Warrior" — which some players may prefer for its more forgiving playstyle and familiar rotation similar to that of a Fury Warrior. While it performs worse than the traditional Arms playstyle with a two-handed weapon on paper (3%-5% lower damage), this easier-to-play rotation is a good alternative for players who struggle to use Slam efficiently, players with poor reaction time, or those with high latency.

This build is mainly viable thanks to the powerful Sword Specialization talent and will naturally be played with two swords, a slow one for the main-hand and a fast one for the off-hand. The axe variant isn't too far behind for Orc Orc Warriors thanks to their Axe Specialization racial.
The best main-hand sword for Phase 1 is the expensive world drop BoE Blinkstrike, which has a chance to grant an extra attack, synergizing particularly well with Sword Specialization and Windfury Totem. However, this weapon has historically been the most expensive BoE of TBC, so it might be an enormous investment for a weapon that is only 0.5% better than the Gladiator's Slicer or 0.8% better than Spiteblade and will be replaced in the next phase already.

The Blacksmithing mace Dragonmaw is so powerful thanks to its insane haste proc that it's the best weapon after Spiteblade despite not even being a sword. It's an expensive weapon to craft but worth considering if you are unlucky with raid drops and Spiteblade keeps eluding you, depending on your server's economy and how much more expensive Blinkstrike sells for at the auction house.

Best in Slot Off Hand Weapons for Arms Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 1
Dual-Wielding Arms performs best with a fast off-hand sword thanks to the Sword Specialization granting you an extra main-hand attack, even when the proc comes from your off-hand. A faster off-hand will ensure steady and reliable procs and perform ~1.2% better than a slow sword with identical stats on single target. Additionally, a fast weapon generally improves your playstyle, thanks to smoother rage generation to ensure an easier and more predictable rotation, and no reason to worry about your swing timers and synced weapons ever.
On the flip side, a slower off-hand will generally perform better on AoE and cleave now that Whirlwind hits with both of your weapons in TBC, so you may want to equip a slow sword against trash mobs, if you can remember to swap weapons around throughout a raid. For a list of slow swords, see the main hand weapons above.

The best fast sword off-hand in every phase is the Gladiator's Quickblade, so hopefully you like TBC arenas. Alternatively, the best PvE option is the surprisingly powerful pre-raid weapon Latro's Shifting Sword, with the fastest speed in the game. Latro's will remain the best off-hand for Phases 1 and 2 due to a lack of raid alternatives.

The Blacksmithing one-handed sword Blazeguard is not an upgrade to Latro's and is not worth spending any gold on. And its Phase 2 evolution Blazefury is merely a 0.3% upgrade over Latro's.

No reason to compete with other classes over such negligible DPS differences, so you can be happy with your green quest reward for the entire phase.

Arms Warrior DPS Best in Slot (BiS) Phase 2 Gear Guide - TBC Classic
By Beanna
Updated: 2026/05/12
Welcome to Wowhead's Best in Slot Gear list from Tempest Keep and Serpentshrine Cavern for Arms Warrior DPS in The Burning Crusade Classic. This guide will list the recommended gear for Arms Warrior DPS to acquire while they progress during the second phase of The Burning Crusade Classic, and contains gear sourced from Tempest Keep and Serpentshrine Cavern, plus other pieces from previous phases like Karazhan, Gruul's Lair, Magtheridon's Lair, heroic dungeons, early PvP grinding, professions, BoE World Drops, and reputations.

We have created many different best in slot lists, including a TBCC pre-patch list and a pre-raid list, containing gear you can obtain without doing any raid content.


Arms Warrior DPS Best in Slot HubArms Warrior DPS Pre-Raid Best in Slot
Best In Slot Gear for Arms Warrior DPS in Phase 2
In Phase 2 of TBCC, the best gear path for an Arms Warrior is to ignore the Tier 5 Set bonuses because the individual items will put you too far above the hit cap, and instead aim for the more contested leather off-set pieces such as Shoulderpads of the Stranger Shoulderpads of the Stranger, Gloves of the Searing Grip Gloves of the Searing Grip and Leggings of Murderous Intent Leggings of Murderous Intent that will cap your expertise for this phase.
However, these leather pieces may be in very high demand in your raid group since they are also BiS for several other melee classes, and achieving a complete gear set may be difficult.

Alternatively, aiming for 4 parts of the Tier 5 Set paired with only one leather off-set piece, such as the shoulders or the legs, is only a minor DPS difference and may be a more reasonable goal in the shorter phases of Anniversary TBC.

    Dual-wielding "Kebab" Arms Warriors should refer to the Fury Warrior BiS Guide for more armor recommendations.

Arms Warriors looking to add Enchants and Gems to their BiS gear should take a look at our guide below:

Arms Warrior Gems & Enchants

Should Arms Warrior DPS Choose Aldor or Scryer for BiS in TBC Classic Phase 2?
Both of the Shattrah factions offer similar shoulders enchants, with a marginal advantage for the Scryers one, providing more Critical Strike rating, more valuable than Attack Power.
The Scryers is also a less expensive reputation to max out, thanks to a lower demand for Sunfury Signet Sunfury Signet and Arcane Tome Arcane Tome compared to the more popular Aldor faction. As a nice bonus, the Scryers also unlocks access to unique Blacksmithing plans that may help you save some gold while leveling Blacksmithing up to lvl 375 to craft your best weapons.

If you need to grind reputation with The Aldor or The Scryers, or any of the other factions that offer BiS gear for Arms Warriors, take a look at our guides to learn the fastest way to reach the rank you'll need.

Best in Slot Trinkets for Arms Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 2
Dragonspine Trophy (DST) is the best trinket in the game and, despite only being a Phase 1 drop, will remain the best trinket up until Wrath of the Lich King for Arms Warriors. Due to the rage mechanic, Warrior naturally scales amazingly with haste for more attacks, leading to more rage, leading to more abilities, and exponential gains throughout the expansion.

Badge of the Swarmguard from Ahn'Qiraj is the best trinket for short encounters up to 1.5 minutes and/or cleave all the way until Phase 4 of TBC.
Armor Penetration is always the best stat for physical classes because your targets take exponential damage the lower their armor. Bosses in TBC have a lot more armor than in Vanilla, and armor reduction effects such as Sunder Armor, Expose Armor, and Faerie Fire remove only about half of it, leaving plenty of armor to strip with Badge of the Swarmguard and Armor Penetration when it starts appearing as a stat on items later into the expansion.
Furthermore, Blizzard announced that all raids will be released in their nerfed state for Anniversary TBC, which means lower bosses' health pool and even shorter encounters, making Badge of the Swarmguard all the more relevant.

Bloodlust Brooch is an okay alternative on-use trinket for short encounters, that can be used in place of Badge for players without the AQ40 trinket, or paired with Badge for players without DST. While it's never as good as Badge for burst and cleave damage, it remains a good trinket to rotate on trash mobs while Badge is on cooldown.

Finally, Phase 2 introduces Tsunami Talisman, a fully passive trinket with excellent stats but best suited for trash mobs or longer encounters over 2 minutes since it lacks the explosive DPS boost of on-use trinkets for short encounters.

All of these trinkets shine in different situations and fight durations, paired with DST. The mark of a great DPS Warrior is to juggle all of them throughout the raid to maximize your burst damage output on trash mobs and to use the relevant secondary trinket for each boss encounter.

    Solarian's Sapphire Solarian's Sapphire is not a great DPS trinket, but it's an important pick up nonetheless to boost the Attack Power provided by your Battle Shout. Paired with 3 parts of Tier 2 for an additional 30 AP in a special gearset to shout before engaging a boss, you can increase the attack power of your Battle Shout by 33% for the whole party, for a substantial ~2.7% DPS increase worth several upgrades.

Best in Slot Two-Handed Weapons for Arms Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 2
As a Two-handed Arms Warrior throughout TBC, you will only want to consider two-handed Swords due to the Sword Specialization talent overshadowing other weapon types. While Orc Orcs can lower the gap with Poleaxe Specialization thanks to their Axe Specialization racial, it's still not enough to beat the amazing Sword Specialization talent.

Phase 2 raids introduce the new Nether Vortex Nether Vortex that will let you upgrade your Blacksmithing weapon Lionheart Champion into its final form: Lionheart Executioner. Same amazing proc, more DPS!
That being said, while LHE is an amazing sword that can be upgraded quickly at the start of Phase 2, it is not technically the best two-handed weapon of this phase. Twinblade of the Phoenix has it beat by only a couple of DPS thanks to its superior item budget and damage range as a Kael'thas drop, and will be your end goal for Phase 2.

    Some players may be tempted to craft the Stormherald mace instead for PvP, but be aware that Mace Specialization provides basically zero benefits to your damage in PvE, and you will lose ~7% of your damage for not using a sword. While that may not sound like much to some, 7% damage is the amount of DPS you gain from going from Phase 1 BiS to Phase 2 BiS. So you are essentially cancelling out an entire phase worth of raid upgrades if you show up to raid with a mace instead of a sword.

Best in Slot Main Hand Weapons for Arms Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 2

While it performs worse than the traditional Arms playstyle with a two-handed weapon on paper (3%-5% lower damage), this easier-to-play rotation is a good alternative for players who struggle to use Slam efficiently, players with poor reaction time, or those with high latency.
This build is mainly viable thanks to the powerful Sword Specialization talent and will naturally be played with two swords, a slow one for the main-hand and a fast one for the off-hand. The axe variant isn't too far behind for Orc Orc Warriors thanks to their Axe Specialization racial.

The best main-hand sword for Phase 2 is Talon of Azshara thanks to its slower 2.7 speed that gives it a small advantage over the Season 2 Merciless Gladiator's Slicer. The expensive world drop BoE Blinkstrike remains competitive for another phase, only 0.5% worse than Talon when paired with a fast off-hand for more consistent procs.

Best in Slot Off Hand Weapons for Arms Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 2
Dual-Wielding Arms performs best with a fast off-hand sword thanks to the Sword Specialization granting you an extra main-hand attack, even when the proc comes from your off-hand. A faster off-hand will ensure steady and reliable procs and perform ~1.2% better than a slow sword with identical stats on single target. Additionally, a fast weapon generally improves your playstyle, thanks to smoother rage generation to ensure an easier and more predictable rotation, and no reason to worry about your swing timers and synced weapons ever.
On the flip side, a slower off-hand will generally perform better on AoE and cleave now that Whirlwind hits with both of your weapons in TBC, so you may want to equip a slow sword against trash mobs, if you can remember to swap weapons around throughout a raid. For a list of slow swords, see the main hand weapons above.

Similarly to Phase 1, the best fast sword off-hand for Phase 2 is the Season 2 Merciless Gladiator's Quickblade, a ~1% upgrade over the Season 1 Gladiator's Quickblade, so hopefully you like TBC arenas because there are no PvE raid alternatives until the next phase. Outside of arenas, the best off-hand remains the blue pre-raid weapon Latro's Shifting Sword for a lack of alternatives.

Burning Crusade Classic Arms Warrior DPS Best in Slot (BiS) Phase 3 Gear Guide
By Beanna
Updated: 2026/07/18
Welcome to Wowhead's Best in Slot Gear list from Phase 3 Black Temple and Mount Hyjal for Arms Warrior DPS in The Burning Crusade Classic. This guide will list the recommended gear for Arms Warrior DPS to acquire while they progress during the third phase of The Burning Crusade Classic, and contains gear sourced from Black Temple and Mount Hyjal, plus other pieces from previous phases like Serpentshrine Cavern, Tempest Keep, heroic dungeons, arena PvP Gear, professions, BoE World Drops, and reputations.
Best In Slot Gear for Arms Warrior DPS in Phase 3
Phase 3 and the Black Temple raid introduce the legendary The Twin Blades of Azzinoth, wielded by Illidan himself, and its incredible set bonuses granting a 10-sec +450 haste proc every 45 seconds on top of a flat +200 AP bonus when fighting demons that already rivals Phase 5 weapons.
This slow main-hand sword + fast off-hand sword combo just happens to be the ideal set of weapons for a dual-wielding "Kebab" Arms Warrior thanks to the Sword Specialization and, as a result, the dual-wield Arms build will theoretically surpass the two-handed "Slam" Arms build for the first time this phase (by ~2.5% DPS). However, I probably don't need to explain how rare and contested this iconic set of Legendary weapons is, and it's very unlikely to be assigned to an Arms Warrior considering Fury Warriors (and Rogues) will gain magnitudes more DPS from these weapons.

On the other hand, the Two-Handed Arms Warrior Best In Slot gear set will be easier to obtain in Phase 3. First off, because its best weapon Cataclysm's Edge Cataclysm's Edge should be uncontested in your raid. But also because it conveniently avoids some of the most sought-after items this phase, such as Cursed Vision of Sargeras Cursed Vision of Sargeras, Choker of Endless Nightmares Choker of Endless Nightmares or Shadowmoon Destroyer's Drape Shadowmoon Destroyer's Drape, thanks to its lower hit requirement.

Should Arms Warrior DPS Choose Aldor or Scryer for BiS in TBC Classic Phase 3?
Both of the Shattrah factions offer similar shoulder enchants, with a marginal advantage for the Scryers one, providing more Critical Strike rating, more valuable than Attack Power.
The Scryers is also a less expensive reputation to max out, thanks to a lower demand for Sunfury Signet Sunfury Signet and Arcane Tome Arcane Tome compared to the more popular Aldor faction, but that may vary based on your server's economy, so make sure to check your local Auction House. As a nice bonus, the Scryers also unlock access to unique Blacksmithing plans that may help you save some gold while leveling Blacksmithing up to level 375 to craft your best weapons.

If you need to grind reputation with The Aldor or The Scryers, or any of the other factions that offer BiS gear for Fury Warriors, take a look at our guides to learn the fastest way to reach the rank you'll need.

Best in Slot Trinkets for Arms Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 3
Dragonspine Trophy (DST) is the best trinket in the game and, despite only being a Phase 1 drop, will remain the best trinket up until Wrath of the Lich King for Arms Warriors. Due to the rage mechanic, Warrior naturally scales amazingly with haste for more attacks, leading to more rage, leading to more abilities, and exponential gains throughout the expansion.

As for your second trinket, the choice is entirely dependent on the encounter ahead and fight duration.

    Badge of the Swarmguard from Ahn'Qiraj only gets better with more Armor Penetration now available on your gear as your DPS increases exponentially the closer to 0 armor your target is. Badge is the best trinket for cleave and encounters shorter than 2 minutes.
    Madness of the Betrayer is a new trinket for Phase 3 with a +300 Armor Penetration proc for 10 seconds. Thanks to a good 1 PPM and no ICD, you can expect a theoretical average uptime of 50%, which makes Madness an excellent passive trinket that will outshine Badge of the Swarmguard for encounters longer than 2 minutes or for trash packs in general. It's the perfect fall-back trinket to default to when Badge is on CD.
    Tsunami Talisman is another excellent passive trinket from Phase 2, only a couple of DPS below Madness, that you would use in the same situations.
    Bloodlust Brooch and Empty Mug of Direbrew are severely outclassed at this stage of the game. They will perform slightly better than passive trinkets for encounters below 1 minute or for trash packs if you have lots of downtimes.

Best in Slot Main Hand Weapons for Arms Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 3
Arms Warrior can also be played as an alternative Dual-Wield build — often nicknamed "Kebab Warrior" — which some players may prefer for its more forgiving playstyle and familiar rotation similar to that of a Fury Warrior. While it performs worse than the traditional Arms playstyle with a two-handed weapon on paper (3%-5% lower damage), this easier-to-play rotation is a good alternative for players who struggle to use Slam efficiently, players with poor reaction time, or those with high latency.
This build is mainly viable thanks to the powerful Sword Specialization talent and will naturally be played with two swords, a slow one for the main-hand and a fast one for the off-hand.

Phase 3 and the Black Temple raid introduce the legendary The Twin Blades of Azzinoth, wielded by Illidan himself, and its incredible set bonuses granting a 10-sec +450 haste proc every 45 seconds on top of a flat +200 AP bonus when fighting demons that already rivals Phase 5 weapons.
This slow main-hand sword + fast off-hand sword combo just happens to be the ideal set of weapons for a dual-wielding "Kebab" Warrior and, as a result, the dual-wield Arms build will theoretically surpass the two-handed "Slam" Arms build for the first time this phase (by ~2.5% DPS). However, I probably don't need to explain how rare and contested this iconic set of Legendary weapons is, and it's very unlikely to be assigned to an Arms Warrior considering Fury Warriors (and Rogues) will gain magnitudes more DPS from these weapons.

Outside of the Warglaives, the best weapons for Phase 3 are the S3 Arena weapons. Indeed, arena weapons this phase benefit from a higher DPS than weapons available in PvE raids thanks to a higher item level. The S3 Vengeful Gladiator's Slicer is the best slow sword for your main-hand and a decent upgrade over Blade of Infamy, the best main-hand sword available outside of PvP.

Best in Slot Off Hand Weapons for Arms Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 3
Dual-Wielding Arms performs best with a fast off-hand sword thanks to the Sword Specialization granting you an extra main-hand attack, even when the proc comes from your off-hand. A faster off-hand will ensure steady and reliable procs and perform ~1% better than a slow sword with identical stats on single target. Additionally, a fast weapon generally improves your playstyle, thanks to smoother rage generation to ensure an easier and more predictable rotation, and no reason to worry about your swing timers and synced weapons ever.
On the flip side, a slower off-hand will generally perform better on AoE and cleave now that Whirlwind hits with both of your weapons in TBC, so you may want to equip a slow sword against trash mobs, if you can remember to swap weapons around throughout a raid. For a list of slow swords, see the main hand weapons above.

Similarly to main-hand weapons, the fast Warglaive of Azzinoth is the best off-hand individually but especially when wielded alongside the main-hand for the powerful set bonuses.
Outside of the legendary sword, the best off-hand is the Blade of Savagery thanks to its superior speed that beats the S3 Arena Vengeful Gladiator's Quickblade only by a couple of DPS.

Welcome to Wowhead's Best in Slot Gear list from Phase 4 Zul'Aman for Arms Warrior DPS in The Burning Crusade Classic. This guide will list the recommended gear for Arms Warrior DPS to acquire while they progress during the fourth phase of The Burning Crusade Classic, and contains gear sourced from Zul'Aman, Black Temple, and Mount Hyjal, plus other pieces from previous phases like Serpentshrine Cavern, Tempest Keep, heroic dungeons, arena PvP Gear, professions, BoE World Drops, and reputations.
Best In Slot Gear for Arms Warrior DPS in Phase 4
Phase 4 will be similar to Phase 3, offering both a Two-Hand and Duel-Wield builds. The builds all have some differences, but the main difference is that you can opt into the 4-set or only 2. Tier 6 comes with 2 unique set bonuses.

The 2 set bonus, Reduced Cleave Cost, acts as a small rage reduction that stacks with our talent, Improved Execute. A minor gain at best, this bonus helps smooth out our execute phase when not under the effects of cooldowns, and in limited rage scenarios. The 4 set bonus, Improved Mortal Strike and Bloodthirst, is much Stronger for Arms than it is for Fury. Mortal Strike makes up a larger percentage of our Damage, and the lack of hit on some Tier pieces means that using them is the preferred option when in a raid with a Boomkin or other source of Improved Faerie Fire.

Arms Warriors looking to add Enchants and Gems to their BiS gear should take a look at our guide below:

Arms Warrior Gems & Enchants

Should Arms Warrior DPS Choose Aldor or Scryer for BiS in TBC Classic Phase 4?
Both of the Shattrah Factions are close in DPS for Warriors, although the Scryer shoulder Enchant is slightly better for personal DPS. However, the difference is minimal, and Aldor comes with a bonus of having an extremely strong MH/OH in Vindicator's Brand Vindicator's Brand. Aldor is also usually more expensive to gain reputation with, due to its status as the most popular faction, and it also has a major downside in the form of a much more expensive Blacksmithing Plan for when you level your professions. In conclusion, although Scryer has a slight numerical advantage, neither faction will make an extreme difference to your personal gameplay.

If you need to grind reputation with The Aldor or The Scryers, or any of the other factions that offer BiS gear for Arms Warriors, take a look at our guides to learn the fastest way to reach the rank you'll need.

Best in Slot Head Armor for Arms Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 4
Cursed Vision of Sargeras is BiS by a solid margin, but can be quite contested as it is BiS for both BM/MM Hunters, Ret Paladins, and Enh Shamans. Do not build sets around these items' Hit Rating unless you can guarantee you will get it. In addition, as Arms has a hard hit cap as opposed to the soft hit cap of Fury, this item can be substantially reduced in DPS gain if the hit is not utilized. Make sure you know your character and gear progression before you take this item!

Both of the other helm options are competitive when taking into account building around either 4Set or Offpieces. Helm of the Illidari Shatterer is almost a warrior exclusive item, and its high Hit Rating can help with making up for missing pieces, while Onslaught Battle-Helm, although wanted by some hunter and shaman specs, can be used to create a moderately strong 4 set bonus.

Both Furious Gizmatic Goggles and Destroyer Battle-Helm remain independently strong items, although you should seek to pick up any of the three Tier 6 helms as an upgrade eventually.

Welcome to Wowhead's Best in Slot Gear list from Phase 5 Sunwell Plateau for Fury Warrior DPS in The Burning Crusade Classic. This guide will list the recommended gear for Fury Warrior DPS to acquire while they progress during the fifth and final phase of The Burning Crusade Classic, which contains gear sourced from Sunwell Plateau, Zul'Aman, Black Temple, and Mount Hyjal, plus other pieces from previous phases like Serpentshrine Cavern, Tempest Keep, heroic dungeons, arena PvP Gear, professions, BoE World Drops, and reputations.
Best In Slot Gear for Arms Warrior DPS in Phase 5
As we approach the final phase of TBC, we get our final best-in-slot guide for Atms Warrior. The gear is so powerful that nearly everything will be replaced from the previous phase, minus Warglaives of Azzinoth, should you be opting into a dual-wield Arms build, and have been lucky enough to obtain them! Since Arms Warrior more traditionally wields two-handed weapons, however, those options will be listed above the one-handed.

The 2 set bonus, Reduced Cleave Cost, acts as a small rage reduction that stacks with our talent, Improved Execute. A minor gain at best, this bonus helps smooth out our execute phase when not under the effects of cooldowns, and in limited rage scenarios.

The 4 set bonus, Improved Mortal Strike and Bloodthirst, is deceptively strong. Although Bloodthirst feels like it deals a large amount of damage, its cooldown and non-benefit from haste cause it to become a smaller amount of damage than anticipated when more than one target is present. However, it remains ok on a single target, and ending up with this bonus by accident is not a huge problem.

Although Crit gems (such as Smooth Lionseye Smooth Lionseye) have dominated the past few phases of gemming, more strength-focused gemming can begin to outscale (and in some cases already has) stacking Critical Strike Rating. I recommend using a simulator to accurately figure out which form of gem is strongest for you based on your kill times/group composition/gear, etc. If in doubt, then the versatile Inscribed Pyrestone Inscribed Pyrestone can be useful as a compromise between the two ideologies of gemming.

Arms Warriors looking to add Enchants and Gems to their BiS gear should take a look at our guide below:

Arms Warrior Gems & Enchants
Should Arms Warrior DPS Choose Aldor or Scryer for BiS in TBC Classic Phase 5?
Both of the Shattrah Factions are close in DPS for Warriors, although the Scryer shoulder Enchant is slightly better for personal DPS. However, the difference is minimal, and Aldor comes with an added bonus of having an extremely strong MH/OH in Vindicator's Brand Vindicator's Brand. Aldor is also usually more expensive to gain reputation with, due to its status as the most popular faction, and it also has a major downside in the form of a much more expensive Blacksmithing Plan for when you level your professions. In conclusion, although Scryer has a slight numerical advantage, neither faction will make an extreme difference to your personal gameplay.

If you need to grind reputation with The Aldor or The Scryers, or any of the other factions that offer BiS gear for Arms Warriors, take a look at our guides to learn the fastest way to reach the rank you'll need.

If you have any favour with your guild, convince them to give you these items - they are the single most powerful upgrades in TBC.

Outside of the Warglaives, Dragonstrike remains our easy and strong option, although S4 Weapons and Muramasa are both slightly better than our old faithful dragon mace.

Aside from Arena Weapons and the Warglaives, Muramasa can be used as an offhand, as well as the fist weapon from trash. If not, you can also use Blade of Infamy
