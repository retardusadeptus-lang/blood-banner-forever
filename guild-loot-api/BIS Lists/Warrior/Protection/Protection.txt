Protection Warrior Tank Best in Slot (BiS) Phase 2 Gear Guide - TBC Classic
By Beanna
Updated: 2026/05/12
Welcome to Wowhead's Best in Slot Gear list from Tempest Keep and Serpentshrine Cavern for Protection Warrior Tank in The Burning Crusade Classic. This guide will list the recommended gear for Protection Warrior Tank to acquire while they progress during the second phase of The Burning Crusade Classic, and contains gear sourced from Tempest Keep and Serpentshrine Cavern, plus other pieces from previous phases like Karazhan, Gruul's Lair, Magtheridon's Lair, heroic dungeons, early PvP grinding, professions, BoE World Drops, and reputations.

We have created many different best in slot lists, including a TBCC pre-patch list and a pre-raid list, containing gear you can obtain without doing any raid content.

Best In Slot Gear for Protection Warrior Tank in Phase 2
Gearing your Protection Warrior in TBCC highly depends on your raiding environment. Some guilds may require a higher threat ceiling to withstand the pressure from high-performing damage dealers, while other guilds may encourage erring on the side of caution and focusing solely on survival. Most guilds will require a little bit of both, and it is up to each tank to decide on the balance between threat and mitigation they want to achieve based on their experience within their group and for different encounters.

For this reason, I have made a selection of the most balanced and budget-efficient items for each slot as well as a threat and a mitigation-oriented alternative when applicable.

It is also important to note that gearing as a Protection Warrior in TBCC requires constant adjustments to maintain your crit immunity as you upgrade your gear, and you may lose Defense or Resilience rating in some slots. Make sure you keep some item alternatives to mix and match your stats and meet your goals for survival and threat.

The Protection Warrior Tier 5 set Destroyer Armor comes with two decent set bonuses.

    2 pieces: Shield Block Block Value Each time you use your Shield Block ability, you gain 100 block value against a single attack in the next 6 sec.
    4 pieces: Battle Rush You have a chance each time you are hit to gain 200 haste rating for 10 sec. (Proc chance: 7%).

Despite the misleading wording, the 2-piece bonus increases your character's block value by 100, which also affects your Shield Slam damage, if you happen to use Shield Slam right after triggering this buff with Shield Block and before the buff is consumed by your next blocked incoming attack. However, Shield Block and Shield Slam's cooldowns do not match and only line up every 30 seconds, which means you can only expect that two Slams per minute will benefit from the block value buff.

The 4-piece bonus Battle Rush, assuming a proc chance of 7% and a boss swing of 2.4 seconds with Improved Thunder Clap, should have an average uptime of ~25%, which amounts to a ~1.3% TPS increase. It's not a lot, but it's a nice threat bonus on top of great items overall that would already be BiS based on stats alone.

Aldor vs. Scryer for Protection Warrior Tank in Phase 2
Choosing between The Aldor or The Scryers determines which shoulders inscription you can access upon reaching exalted, which are basically equal for either side. But more importantly, your choice will affect the proc of your Shattered Sun Offensive necklaces in Phase 5, which should have a greater influence on your decision.

    The Inscriptions
    Mathematically speaking, the Aldor's inscription Greater Inscription of Warding Greater Inscription of Warding totals slightly more raw avoidance at 1.30% avoidance, while the Scryers' Greater Inscription of the Knight Greater Inscription of the Knight provides 1.28% avoidance. This minuscule difference, at the scale of your entire gear set, is only a 0.06% difference in total mitigation value, which is to say negligible and irrelevant.
    Math aside, it is worth mentioning that more defense can help facilitate your gearing process early on in Phase 1. This can be a good argument for choosing the Scryers, as you replace a lot of gear fast and have to adjust your crit immunity constantly.

    The Necklace
    The Shattered Sun Offensive tank necklace, Shattered Sun Pendant of Resolve Shattered Sun Pendant of Resolve, available in Phase 5 once you are exalted with this new faction, comes in two versions based on your chosen Shattrath faction. The Aldor version has a +100 dodge rating proc (+5.28% dodge) and the Scryers version has a +100 expertise rating proc (-6.25% dodge/parry).
    While the Aldor necklace may look like the more survival-oriented choice at first glance, the Scryers version is in fact both the best of the two for threat by a landslide but also for damage mitigation thanks to the additional expertise reducing your chances to parry haste bosses, thus resulting in higher survivability than the dodge proc.

In conclusion, the Scryers is the obvious choice for a Warrior Tank since it offers the best shoulders inscription and the best necklace in Phase 5. Additionally, it also offers exclusive Blacksmithing plans that can save you a lot of gold when leveling that profession. And as a final bonus, it's also generally the less expensive faction of the two to max out, thanks to its lower popularity.

If you need to grind reputation with The Aldor or The Scryers, or any of the other factions that offer BiS gear for Protection Warriors, take a look at our guides to learn the fastest way to reach the rank you'll need.

Best in Slot Ring Jewelry for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 2
Shapeshifter's Signet remains by far the best ring available and your recommended first ring of choice until Phase 4 for its large amount of expertise, which greatly increases your threat, pull variance, and damage mitigation indirectly by reducing parry haste.
If you need hit rating, Violet Signet of the Master Assassin is an excellent choice paired with Shapeshifter's Signet. However, if you are looking to trim both hit and defense rating on your gear set, consider Ancestral Ring of Conquest as a great, balanced item with little to no contest.

Other threat-oriented rings in Phase 2, unfortunately, overlap with most physical damage dealers' BiS gear and will be highly contested. As always, it is well regarded to work with your raid team and pass on contested items when you can get by just fine with slightly worse alternatives.

As for mitigation options, Phase 2 tank rings are rather disappointing. Seventh Ring of the Tirisfalen comes with a dead stat in the form of block rating and Ring of Sundered Souls with not-so-desirable dodge rating. Both are worse mitigation pieces than the bonus armor Phase 1 rings and are not worth pursuing.

The only mitigation upgrade to the excellent bonus armor rings Violet Signet of the Great Protector, A'dal's Signet of Defense, or Shermanar Great-Ring, is the first tier of the tank Band of Eternity linked to the Hyjal raid reputation, which you can obtain as a quest reward after completing the Hyjal Summit attunement quest which increases your The Scale of the Sands reputation to Friendly in the same time.

    As a tank, it is essential that you pick the Defender's Pledge Defender's Pledge quest to obtain the tank Band of Eternity Band of Eternity and, later, the Revered version Band of Eternity Band of Eternity to keep until Phase 4.
    The reason why you will want to keep the Revered ring instead of the Exalted version, Band of the Eternal Defender Band of the Eternal Defender, is because the 10-second armor proc overwrites your Scroll of Protection V Scroll of Protection V, leading to worse damage mitigation than the Revered version, same stats minus the undesirable proc. This was reported in 2021-2022 and confirmed not a bug.
    The trick is to progress the tank ring quest chain up to Revered. Delete your Revered tank ring and ask Soridormi for a new one. Once you reach Exalted with The Scale of the Sands, upgrade your Revered ring to the Exalted version and immediately change it to the DPS ring Band of the Eternal Champion Band of the Eternal Champion. Then you can item restore your Revered tank ring to keep this snapshotted version forever and never upgrade it. If you pick any other path first, there is no way to get the Revered tank ring, and you will be stuck with the bad Exalted one.

Best in Slot Trinkets for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 2
Trinkets are an ideal slot to adjust your threat or mitigation needs on the fly with a large variety of options ranging from pure threat to pure stamina. It is generally advised to avoid relying on trinkets to achieve your crit immunity to preserve this freedom of juggling different options based on the encounter.

For mitigation, the only noteworthy item added with Phase 2 is Spyglass of the Hidden Fleet. It comes with less stamina than the amazing engineering trinkets Goblin Rocket Launcher and Gnomish Poultryizer, and has a rather underwhelming on-use effect. Still a great pick-up if you aren't an engineer and an upgrade to Argussian Compass or Violet Badge nonetheless, but I highly recommend choosing this profession for the many tools it provides to help your AoE threat generation.
A personal favorite of mine is Timelapse Shard because its high resilience rating provides a lot of liberty in your gear choices without losing your crit immunity. It's especially handy for resistance sets with crafted gear, which provides little to no defense.

The best threat-oriented trinket for Phase 2 remains the Icon of Unyielding Courage, providing a large chunk of hit rating but, more importantly, an on-use that will boost your armor penetration to build that essential snap threat on pull. Hit rating, Expertise, and Armor penetration are the strongest offensive stats for a Protection Warrior, since it increases the damage output of your abilities across the board, even the odd ones like Revenge or Shield Slam.
Alternatively, Romulo's Poison Vial is a close second choice with slightly more hit rating, trading the more explosive threat boost of Icon's on-use for a slower, steadier threat increase with its poison damage proc.

As a third choice, the Gnomeregan Auto-Blocker 600 is a great, balanced trinket with both threat and mitigation value and a middle-ground between the Engie trinkets and Icon. Since it is considered a defensive trinket, the on-use does not share a cooldown with offensive trinkets such as Icon, which means you can pair both buffs for maximum snap threat on pull.

Best in Slot Weapons for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 2
Similarly to Phase 1, the best tank weapon overall for Phase 2 is still the Blacksmithing mace Dragonmaw and its evolution Dragonstrike.
A fast weapon usually benefits a Protection Warrior more to convert more rage into Heroic Strikes, as long as incoming damage is high enough to fill your rage bar consistently. However, Blizzard announced that the Serpentshrine Cavern and Tempest Keep raids will yet again be released in their nerfed state, leading to far less threatening encounters and incoming damage, barring a couple of exceptions. As a result, a slower weapon will perform better for the majority of the raid content due to limited rage, and Dragonstrike in particular will shine thanks to its high damage range and powerful haste proc with no ICD that synergize especially well with Devastate doubling proc chances when applying Sunder Armor.

The iconic Mallet of the Tides can still be a competitive weapon for threat output against a handful of the most threatening bosses, whose damage dealt will give you enough rage to take advantage of its high speed. But these situations favoring fast weapons will be rarer in nerfed content, and fast weapons in general will underperform on the majority of encounters and trash mobs, making Mallet of the Tides more situational and a less versatile choice than Dragonstrike. The more defensive stats on Mallet barely amount to a 1% damage reduction compared to Dragonstrike.

Although daggers are generally a poor choice of weapons for a Protection Warrior due to their lower attack power normalization crippling your Devastate damage, Fang of Vashj manages to creep up in 4th place thanks to its far superior item level and damage range already equal to Phase 3 weapons due to being a drop from the final boss of this phase. That being said, it's merely a 1% upgrade over Karazhan weapons and still a worse option than Dragonmaw, but I had to mention it to clear up any confusion.

Beanna <Guide Author> on 2021/09/18 (Burning Crusade)
Impale has always been a tempting talent for protection tanks since vanilla, but because of its position in the arms tree, it requires taking points away from Cruelty to reach, which ends up being a net TPS and DPS loss below high crit chances for a tank in the ~25% range.
While you can technically reach 25% crit using a more offensive gear set and Elixir of Major Agility, the TPS gain from Impale remains quite marginal (<1%) and comes at the expense of pull variance (Cruelty) and mitigation (Shield Mastery). Furthermore, such offensive gear can not realistically be used on every encounter, leaving you with inferior talents the majority of the time.
The same reasoning applies to Relentless Earthstorm Diamond. You would need a high crit chance in the 25% range as well for RED to match the Powerful Earthstorm Diamond in stats value alone, but the requirements of 2 reds and 2 yellows completely plunge the EP value of the meta gem compared to using a Solid Star of Elune in every socket. While you could slightly raise your DPS and TPS this way, the sacrifice on your EHP is not a worthwhile trade-off as a tank.
For example, with your gear set @13.17% crit unbuffed, each talent point in Impale is 35% less TPS than a point in Cruelty and overall 47% less EP than a point in Shield Mastery. While the description of Impale can easily mislead you into thinking you would gain a threat advantage by investing in this talent, you are in fact losing threat compared to 5/5 Cruelty because your gear choices are not coherent to benefit from Impale and/or RED.
I recommend using the excellent Tank spreadsheet by Emrus that you can find in the Fight Club discord for a better understanding of the value of each talent, gem, enchant, buff, etc. It's very well made and intuitive to use!

Protection Warrior Tank Phase 1 Best in Slot (BiS) - TBC Classic
By Beanna
Updated: 2026/03/28
Welcome to Wowhead's Best in Slot Gear list from Karazhan, Gruul's Lair, and Magtheridon's Lair for Protection Warrior Tank in The Burning Crusade Classic. This guide will list the recommended gear for Protection Warrior Tank to acquire while they progress on the first few raids in The Burning Crusade Classic, and contains gear sourced from Karazhan, Gruul's Lair, Magtheridon's Lair, heroic dungeons, early PvP grinding, professions, BoE World Drops, and reputations.

We have created many different best-in-slot lists, including a pre-raid list, containing gear you can obtain without doing any raid content.


Protection Warrior Tank Best in Slot Guides
Best In Slot Gear for Protection Warrior Tank in Phase 1
Gearing your Protection Warrior in TBCC highly depends on your raiding environment. Some guilds may require a higher threat ceiling to withstand the pressure from high-performing damage dealers, while other guilds may encourage erring on the side of caution and focusing solely on survival. Most guilds will require a little bit of both, and it is up to each tank to decide on the balance between threat and mitigation they want to achieve based on their experience within their group and for different encounters.

For this reason, I have made a selection of the most balanced and budget-efficient items for each slot as well as a threat and a mitigation-oriented alternative when applicable.

It is also important to note that gearing as a Protection Warrior in TBCC requires constant adjustments to maintain your crit immunity as you upgrade your gear, and you may lose Defense or Resilience rating in some slots. Make sure you keep some item alternatives to mix and match your stats and meet your goals for survival and threat.

The Protection Warrior Tier 4 set Warbringer Armor comes with very mediocre set bonuses.

    2 pieces: Blade Turning 25% chance to gain a 200 damage absorption shield after a parry is a negligible amount of damage mitigation.
    4 pieces: Revenge Bonus increasing the damage of your next ability by 10% after using Revenge yields very little threat because this next spell is always going to be Devastate or Heroic Strike, abilities with low base damage.

For this reason, we are going to focus on individual items for each slot instead of trying to activate any of the T4 bonuses specifically with worse items.

Protection Warriors looking to add Enchants and Gems to their BiS gear should take a look at our guide below:

Protection Warrior Gems & Enchants

Should Protection Warrior Tank Choose Aldor or Scryer for BiS in TBC Classic Phase 1?
Choosing between The Aldor or The Scryers determines which shoulders inscription you can access upon reaching exalted, which are basically equal for either side. But more importantly, your choice will affect the proc of your Shattered Sun Offensive necklaces in Phase 5, which should have a greater influence on your decision.

    The Inscriptions
    Mathematically speaking, the Aldor's inscription Greater Inscription of Warding Greater Inscription of Warding totals slightly more raw avoidance at 1.30% avoidance, while the Scryers' Greater Inscription of the Knight Greater Inscription of the Knight provides 1.28% avoidance. This minuscule difference, at the scale of your entire gear set, is only a 0.06% difference in total mitigation value, which is to say negligible and irrelevant.
    Math aside, it is worth mentioning that more defense can help facilitate your gearing process early on in Phase 1. This can be a good argument for choosing the Scryers, as you replace a lot of gear fast and have to adjust your crit immunity constantly.

    The Necklace
    The Shattered Sun Offensive tank necklace, Shattered Sun Pendant of Resolve Shattered Sun Pendant of Resolve, available in Phase 5 once you are exalted with this new faction, comes in two versions based on your chosen Shattrath faction. The Aldor version has a +100 dodge rating proc (+5.28% dodge) and the Scryers version has a +100 expertise rating proc (-6.25% dodge/parry).
    While the Aldor necklace may look like the more survival-oriented choice at first glance, the Scryers version is in fact both the best of the two for threat by a landslide but also for damage mitigation thanks to the additional expertise reducing your chances to parry haste bosses, thus resulting in higher survivability than the dodge proc.

In conclusion, the Scryers is the obvious choice for a Warrior Tank since it offers the best shoulders inscription and the best necklace in Phase 5. Additionally, it also offers exclusive Blacksmithing plans that can save you a lot of gold when leveling that profession. And as a final bonus, it's also generally the less expensive faction of the two to max out, thanks to its lower popularity.

If you need to grind reputation with The Aldor or The Scryers, or any of the other factions that offer BiS gear for Protection Warriors, take a look at our guides to learn the fastest way to reach the rank you'll need.

Best in Slot Head Armor for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 1
Warbringer Greathelm, the Tier 4 Greathelm is a very well itemized piece for mitigation, but also comes with a moderate amount of threat in the form of agility and block value. While this may not be the most offensive choice, it is definitely the highest overall value helm for phase 1. However, there are several good mitigation-oriented alternatives for this slot, so do not stress about acquiring a T4 helm token immediately. If you are into Arenas or want to go the extra mile, the Gladiator's Plate Helm is the best threat-oriented helmet available. Especially when paired with the shoulders or the chestpiece to activate the 2p set bonus Increased Resilience 35, that is a substantial boost towards your crit immunity.

Best in Slot Trinkets for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 1
Trinkets are an ideal slot to adjust your threat or mitigation needs on the fly with a large variety of options ranging from pure threat to pure stamina. Best in Slot Weapons for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 1
Your choice of weapon is mainly dictated by your rage generation on a specific encounter. For this reason, there isn't really a single best weapon for all situations.

    Against low to medium hitting enemies, such as normal dungeons and nerfed Karazhan bosses, your rage generation from incoming damage is expected to be low, and thus you won't be able to use Heroic Strike often. In this situation, a slow main-hand weapon will boost your Devastate damage the most and yield the highest Threat Per Second (TPS).
    Against hard-hitting enemies, such as High King Maulgar or Magtheridon, your rage bar will be constantly full from the incoming damage, which will let you convert every swing into a Heroic Strike. In this situation, a fast main-hand will yield more total Heroic Strike, from which the base threat value will greatly increase your TPS and far outweigh the slightly lower Devastate damage.
    Finally, in Heroic dungeons, where your rage generation from incoming damage will be high but you will also use Cleave the most to build threat on multiple targets, a slow weapon will be preferred for higher Cleave damage, and optionally Whirlwind.

On paper, an ideal combo of weapons for Phase 1 would be the slow Blacksmithing craft Dragonmaw for its very high damage and amazing haste proc, which doubles as the best weapon for Fury Warrior, should you be asked to DPS at any point. Paired with a faster weapon such as King's Defender or The Sun Eater for the occasional hard-hitting boss that will fill your rage bar constantly.
That being said, due to underwhelming boss damage and low incoming rage in nerfed Phase 1 raids, Dragonmaw is the best all-rounder weapon for Phase 1, and you will have close to no opportunity to make use of a fast weapon. However, you should still pick up the fast King's Defender in Karazhan just in case future phases come out with pre-nerf content that isn't a complete snoozefest.

    While it may be tempting to use Daggers like in vanilla for their combined good speed, stats, and low demand, they are no longer a viable choice for Protection Warriors due to their unique attack power coefficient normalization (1.7 vs 2.4 for other one-handed weapons), resulting in a significant damage and threat loss with Devastate.

Protection Warrior Tank Pre-Raid Best in Slot (BiS) - TBC Classic
By Beanna
Updated: 2026/02/13
In this Pre-Raid Best-in-Slot Gear List for Protection Warrior Tank in The Burning Crusade Classic, we will list the recommended gear for Protection Warrior Tank to aim for when gearing up to raids in The Burning Crusade Classic, including gear sourced from Normal and Heroic Dungeons, professions, BoE World Drops, and reputations.


Protection Warrior Tank Best in Slot Hub
Pre-Raid Best In Slot Gear for Protection Warrior Tank in Phase 1
Gearing your Protection Warrior in TBC Classic highly depends on your raiding environment. Some guilds may require a higher threat ceiling to withstand the pressure from high-performing damage dealers, while other guilds may encourage erring on the side of caution and focusing solely on survival. Most guilds will require a little bit of both, and it is up to each tank to decide on the balance between threat and mitigation they want to achieve based on their experience within their group and for different encounters. For this reason, I have made a selection of the most balanced and budget-efficient items for each slot as well as a threat and a mitigation-oriented alternative when applicable.

In this pre-raid Best in Slot gear list, you will mainly find recommendations of survival-oriented items to ensure that you, as a tank, aren't hindering the progression of your first raid into Karazhan. As you later obtain upgrades in raids and Heroic dungeons, you will be able to achieve the target survival and crit immunity using fewer mitigation-focused items, and progressively replace those with threat options. Threat should not be a concern during the first couple weeks of release and until DPS classes start scaling with better gear.

While a Protection Warrior is technically safe from incoming critical hits and crushing blows against the majority of bosses as long as you use Shield Block on cooldown, some encounters in Phase 1 such as Prince Malchezaar and Magtheridon could occasionally deplete all your Shield Block charges and catch you offguard. For this reason, we will still aim for passive critical strike immunity with a combination of defense and resilience rating on your gear at all times. Make sure to always keep a few gear pieces with defense or resilience rating in your bank to adjust your gear set and maintain your critical strike immunity as you upgrade with new items.

Note on gems: While you will find Solid Star of Elune Solid Star of Elune as the recommended gem for every item, it is also perfectly acceptable to use the budget version Solid Azure Moonstone Solid Azure Moonstone in your pre-raid gear set based on your server economy.

Protection Warriors looking to add Enchants and Gems to their BiS gear should take a look at our guide below:

Protection Warrior Gems & Enchants
Should Protection Warrior Tank Choose Aldor or Scryer for BiS in TBC Classic Phase 1?
Choosing between The Aldor or The Scryers determines which shoulders inscription you can access upon reaching exalted, which are basically equal for either side. But more importantly, your choice will affect the proc of your Shattered Sun Offensive necklaces in Phase 5, which should have a greater influence on your decision.

    The Inscriptions
    Mathematically speaking, the Aldor's inscription Greater Inscription of Warding Greater Inscription of Warding totals slightly more raw avoidance at 1.30% avoidance, while the Scryers' Greater Inscription of the Knight Greater Inscription of the Knight provides 1.28% avoidance. This minuscule difference, at the scale of your entire gear set, is only a 0.06% difference in total mitigation value, which is to say negligible and irrelevant.
    Math aside, it is worth mentioning that more defense can help facilitate your gearing process early on in Phase 1. This can be a good argument for choosing the Scryers, as you replace a lot of gear fast and have to adjust your crit immunity constantly.

    The Necklace
    The Shattered Sun Offensive tank necklace, Shattered Sun Pendant of Resolve Shattered Sun Pendant of Resolve, available in Phase 5 once you are exalted with this new faction, comes in two versions based on your chosen Shattrath faction. Pre-Raid Best in Slot Trinkets for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 1
Trinkets are an ideal slot to adjust your threat or mitigation needs on the fly with a large variety of options ranging from pure threat to pure stamina. It is generally advised to avoid relying on trinkets to achieve your crit immunity to preserve this freedom of juggling different choices based on the encounter. For mitigation, the stars of Phase 1 are undoubtedly the Engineering trinkets Goblin Rocket Launcher and Gnomish Poultryizer, providing an impressive +45 stamina. These trinkets will remain the best survival trinkets all the way to Phase 3. Plus, they are not unique so you can craft 2 for +90 stamina. The Rocket Launcher doubles as a decent long-range pulling tool, but is a lot more expensive to craft than the Gnomish version. Note: Do not forget to craft the Gnomish only BoP Gnomish Battle Chicken Gnomish Battle Chicken when you are leveling Engineering if you plan to go Goblin later for the Rocket Launcher.
Non-Engineers can use Argussian Compass and Timelapse Shard as alternative stamina trinkets, also providing a nice boost of resilience to reach your crit immunity with minimal gear at the start of the phase. However, I highly recommend becoming an Engineer in TBCC as a Protection Warrior to craft these amazing trinkets as soon as possible, as well as many tools to help your AoE threat generation, and BiS helmets and guns in future phases.

The best threat-oriented trinket for Phase 1 is the Icon of Unyielding Courage, providing a large chunk of hit rating but, more importantly, an on-use that will boost your armor penetration to build that essential snap threat on pull. Armor penetration is one of the strongest offensive stats for a Protection Warrior, since it increases the damage output of your abilities across the board, even the odd ones like Revenge or Shield Slam. On the flip side, Heroic Blood Furnace is one of the hardest heroics to complete with a stupidly hard gauntlet event, so you may not be able to get your hands on this trinket right away...

As a third choice, the Gnomeregan Auto-Blocker 600 is a great, balanced trinket with both threat and mitigation value and a middle-ground between the Engie trinkets and Icon. Since it is considered a defensive trinket, the on-use does not share a cooldown with offensive trinkets such as Icon, which means you can pair both buffs for maximum snap threat on pull.

Because Protection Warrior's abilities don't scale well with offensive stats overall, the best way to increase your threat with trinkets is to raise your hit and expertise rating to make sure you land as many of your attacks as possible. Drake Fang Talisman or Terokkar Tablet of Precision are decent ways to increase your hit chance, but the value of these trinkets is quite low compared to the trinkets mentioned above.

Pre-Raid Best in Slot Weapons for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 1
Your choice of weapon is mainly dictated by your rage generation on a specific encounter. For this reason, there isn't really a single best weapon for all situations.

    Against low to medium hitting enemies, such as normal dungeons and nerfed Karazhan bosses, your rage generation from incoming damage is expected to be low and thus you won't be able to use Heroic Strike often. In this situation, a slow main-hand weapon will boost your Devastate damage the most and yield the highest Threat Per Second (TPS).
    Against hard-hitting enemies, such as High King Maulgar or Magtheridon, your rage bar will be constantly full from the high incoming damage, which will let you convert every swing into a Heroic Strike. In this situation, a fast main-hand will yield more total Heroic Strike, from which the base threat value will greatly increase your TPS and far outweigh the slightly lower Devastate damage.
    Finally, in Heroic dungeons, where your rage generation from incoming damage will be high but you will also use Cleave the most to build threat on multiple targets, a slow weapon will be preferred for higher Cleave damage, and optionally Whirlwind.

On paper, an ideal combo of weapons for Phase 1 would be the slow Blacksmithing craft Dragonmaw for its very high damage and amazing haste proc, which doubles as the best weapon for Fury Warrior, should you be asked to DPS at any point. Paired with a faster weapon such as The Sun Eater, Terokk's Nightmace or Latro's Shifting Sword for the occasional hard-hitting boss that will fill your rage bar constantly.
That being said, if you can't be bothered to change weapons based on the situation, let alone enchant multiple weapons with the expensive Enchant Weapon - Mongoose, then Dragonmaw is the best all-rounder for Phase 1 due to underwhelming boss damage and low incoming rage in nerfed Karazhan.

Despite popular belief, Thunderfury, Blessed Blade of the Windseeker Thunderfury, Blessed Blade of the Windseeker is no longer relevant in TBCC. The threat generated by the proc has been nerfed by ~60%, and while Devastate has double the chances to proc it, it does not make up for its low lvl 60 weapon damage and lack of stats. Thunderfury is worse than pretty much any pre-raid blue weapon.

    While it may be tempting to use Daggers like in vanilla for their combined good speed, stats, and low demand, they are no longer a viable choice for Protection Warriors due to their unique attack power coefficient normalization (1.7 vs 2.4 for other one-handed weapons), resulting in lower rage generation and a significant damage and threat loss with Devastate.

Burning Crusade Classic Protection Warrior Tank Best in Slot (BiS) Phase 3 Gear Guide
By Beanna
Updated: 2026/07/19
Welcome to Wowhead's Best in Slot Gear list from Phase 3 Black Temple and Mount Hyjal for Protection Warrior Tank in The Burning Crusade Classic. This guide will list the recommended gear for Protection Warrior Tank to acquire while they progress during the third phase of The Burning Crusade Classic, and contains gear sourced from Black Temple and Mount Hyjal, plus other pieces from previous phases like Serpentshrine Cavern, Tempest Keep, heroic dungeons, arena PvP Gear, professions, BoE World Drops, and reputations.
Best In Slot Gear for Protection Warrior Tank in Phase 3
Gearing your Protection Warrior in TBCC highly depends on your raiding environment. Some guilds may require a higher threat ceiling to withstand the pressure from high-performing damage dealers, while other guilds may encourage erring on the side of caution and focusing solely on survival. Most guilds will require a little bit of both, and it is up to each tank to decide on the balance between threat and mitigation they want to achieve based on their experience within their group and for different encounters.

For this reason, I have made a selection of the most balanced and budget-efficient items for each slot as well as a threat and a mitigation-oriented alternative when applicable.

It is also important to note that gearing as a Protection Warrior in TBCC requires constant adjustments to maintain your crit immunity as you upgrade your gear, and may lose Defense or Resilience rating in some slots. Make sure you keep some item alternatives to mix and match your stats and meet your goals for survival and threat.

The Protection Warrior Tier 6 set Onslaught Armor comes with two good set bonuses.

    2 pieces: Improved Commanding Shout Increases the health bonus from your Commanding Shout ability by 170.
    4 pieces: Improved Shield Slam Increases the damage of your Shield Slam ability by 10%.

The Protection Tier 6 set bonuses are quite strong and desirable in a vacuum, but sadly, the items themselves aren't. The Protection T6 itemization loses a lot of strength and agility compared to Tier 5 and, instead, focuses primarily on Stamina and mitigation-oriented secondary stats, notably Shield Block Rating (on 4 out of 5 pieces), which is the least desirable stat as a Protection Warrior. As a result, the Protection T6 items all have a poorly distributed item budget and end up being worse items than their T5 equivalent, which completely negates the value of the set bonuses.

On the other hand, the Fury version of the Tier 6 comes with excellent stats to increase our threat, notably the new addition of Armor Penetration, which is one of the rare stats that affects all our spells across the board.

However, there are two important caveats to the use of the Fury Tier 6. First, the lack of defense rating or resilience on so many items makes it difficult to maintain your critical strikes immunity. And second, we now share our token Helm of the Forgotten Protector Helm of the Forgotten Protector with two of the most represented classes in the TBC meta, which can account for up to half of the raid in some guilds and make Tier 6 tokens more contested than previously.

For these reasons, Phase 3 is the phase where it is the most important that you take advantage of the free Season 3 Arena gear to put together your BiS gear set. Not only are several of the S3 armor pieces BiS for their stats alone, but they also come with an enormous amount of Resilience that makes reaching your critical strike immunity all the easier.
I can not insist enough on the benefits of taking advantage of this gear path that is not only guaranteed (one armor piece every 4-5 weeks by simply playing 10 games in a 5v5 team with your friends), but also reduces the competition on T6 tokens for the benefit of your entire raid.

Aldor vs. Scryer for Protection Warrior Tank in Phase 3
Choosing between The Aldor or The Scryers determines which shoulders inscription you can access upon reaching exalted, which are basically equal for either side. But more importantly, your choice will affect the proc of your Shattered Sun Offensive necklaces in Phase 5, which should have a greater influence on your decision.

    The Inscriptions
    Mathematically speaking, the Aldor's inscription Greater Inscription of Warding Greater Inscription of Warding totals slightly more raw avoidance at 1.30% avoidance, while the Scryers' Greater Inscription of the Knight Greater Inscription of the Knight provides 1.28% avoidance. This minuscule difference, at the scale of your entire gear set, is only a 0.06% difference in total mitigation value, which is to say negligible and irrelevant.
    Math aside, it is worth mentioning that more defense can help facilitate your gearing process. This can be a good argument for choosing the Scryers, as you replace a lot of gear fast and have to adjust your crit immunity constantly.

    The Necklace
    The Shattered Sun Offensive tank necklace, Shattered Sun Pendant of Resolve Shattered Sun Pendant of Resolve, available in Phase 5 once you are exalted with this new faction, comes in two versions based on your chosen Shattrath faction. The Aldor version has a +100 dodge rating proc (+5.28% dodge) and the Scryers version has a +100 expertise rating proc (-6.25% dodge/parry).
    While the Aldor necklace may look like the more survival-oriented choice at first glance, the Scryers version is in fact both the best of the two for threat by a landslide but also for damage mitigation thanks to the additional expertise reducing your chances to parry haste bosses, thus resulting in higher survivability than the dodge proc.

In conclusion, the Scryers is the obvious choice for a Warrior Tank since it offers the best shoulders inscription and the best necklace in Phase 5. Additionally, it also offers exclusive Blacksmithing plans that can save you a lot of gold when leveling that profession. And as a final bonus, it's also generally the less expensive faction of the two to max out, thanks to its lower popularity.

If you need to grind reputation with The Aldor or The Scryers, or any of the other factions that offer BiS gear for Protection Warriors, take a look at our guides to learn the fastest way to reach the rank you'll need.

Best in Slot Head Armor for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 3
Despite its low threat value, Faceplate of the Impenetrable is an excellent tank helmet, thanks to its inflated item budget by virtue of being an Illidan drop, and is by far the best helmet for damage mitigation. While you may not need this high amount of mitigation for Phase 3 raids, it is still worth picking up to build a high mitigation set for the deadly Brutallus in Phase 5.

For a more offensive approach, Vengeful Gladiator's Plate Helm is an amazing helmet for threat value with a large amount of stam and resilience that will greatly facilitate maintaining your crit immunity this phase.
On the other hand, the lack of defense or resilience on the T6 Fury set Onslaught Battle-Helm and Helm of the Illidari Shatterer make these offensive choices a lot more difficult to fit in a tank gear set while also maintaining your crit immunity.

The T6 Onslaught Greathelm is unfortunately worse than the T5 helm and a complete waste of the contested Helm of the Forgotten Protector Helm of the Forgotten Protector tier tokens. Both helmets share almost identical primary stats despite being from two different phases and T6 having a higher item level, because the T6 helm has a dead stat in the form of block rating.

Best in Slot Trinkets for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 3
Trinkets are an ideal slot to adjust your threat or mitigation needs on the fly with a large variety of options ranging from pure threat to pure Stamina. It is generally advised to avoid relying on trinkets to achieve your crit immunity to preserve this freedom of juggling different choices based on the encounter.

For mitigation, Phase 3 introduces the very coveted Darkmoon Card: Vengeance and Darkmoon Card: Madness from the new Darkmoon Faire decks. The former is especially sought after because of its proc, which provides an additional ~10-15 TPS. Stamina trinkets are always a great choice for a Protection Warrior because they provide the most value in this slot by a long shot and let you use better, more offensive items in other slots once your survival is assured.

If you can't afford Darkmoon Faire decks, your best options remain the various +45 Stam trinkets. Commander's Badge, Balebrew Charm, and Brightbrew Charm especially are pretty strong because of the various guardians they summon. While their damage doesn't contribute to your threat, they can still do some respectable DPS for guardians during their 30-40 second activation time.

Shadowmoon Insignia is a very niche trinket. It provides very high Defense and Avoidance, but the on-use effect Protector's Vigor is rather lackluster. An extra 1750 HP for 20 sec on a 20-21k HP buffed tank, against bosses swinging at you for ~6k damage, is so minuscule that it probably won't save you, ever. For comparison, the HP it provides is less than 30% of your Last Stand and also worse than a Nightmare Seed Nightmare Seed. I can't think of a single situation where I would personally want this one-use effect over the permanent +590 HP of a Darmoon trinket. While the defense may come in handy if you find yourself struggling to reach your crit immunity, for example, when building a resistance set, Timelapse Shard still provides the same critical strike chance reduction with Stamina on top.

Because your abilities don't scale very well with offensive stats overall, the best way to increase your threat with trinkets is to raise your hit and expertise rating to make sure you land as many of your attacks as possible. Icon of Unyielding Courage or Romulo's Poison Vial are still the best threat trinkets available in Phase 3. Icon provides a nice snap threat boost on pull when you land your first couple rotations of abilities under the effect of Armor Penetration, while Romulo's poison proc adds a more steady damage source equivalent to ~12 TPS.

If you don't need this amount of hit rating, yet still want to use a threat trinket over a Stamina one, your remaining options are to compete with DPS for very coveted upgrades such as Dragonspine Trophy, Madness of the Betrayer or Tsunami Talisman. I simply can not recommend ever pursuing these items, considering the poor effectiveness on a tank instead of a DPS that would get triple the value out of each one of these big-ticket trinkets.

Burning Crusade Classic Protection Warrior Tank Best in Slot (BiS) Phase 4 Gear Guide
By Beanna
Updated: 2026/02/24
Welcome to Wowhead's Best in Slot Gear list from Phase 4 Zul'Aman for Protection Warrior Tank in The Burning Crusade Classic. This guide will list the recommended gear for Protection Warrior Tank to acquire while they progress during the fourth phase of The Burning Crusade Classic, and contains gear sourced from Zul'Aman, Black Temple, and Mount Hyjal, plus other pieces from previous phases like Serpentshrine Cavern, Tempest Keep, heroic dungeons, arena PvP Gear, professions, BoE World Drops, and reputations.
Best In Slot Gear for Protection Warrior Tank in Phase 4
Gearing your Protection Warrior in TBCC highly depends on your raiding environment. Some guilds may require a higher threat ceiling to withstand the pressure from high-performing damage dealers, while other guilds may encourage erring on the side of caution and focusing solely on survival. Most guilds will require a little bit of both, and it is up to each tank to decide on the balance between threat and mitigation they want to achieve based on their experience within their group and for different encounters.

For this reason, I have made a selection of the most balanced and budget-efficient items for each slot as well as a threat and a mitigation-oriented alternative when applicable.

It is also important to note that gearing as a Protection Warrior in TBCC requires constant adjustments to maintain your crit immunity as you upgrade your gear, and you may lose Defense or Resilience rating in some slots. Make sure you keep some item alternatives to mix and match your stats and meet your goals for survival and threat.

The Protection Warrior Tier 6 set Onslaught Armor comes with two good set bonuses.

    2 pieces: Improved Commanding Shout Increases the health bonus from your Commanding Shout ability by 170.
    4 pieces: Improved Shield Slam Increases the damage of your Shield Slam ability by 10%.

The Protection Tier 6 set bonuses are quite strong and desirable in a vacuum, but sadly, the items themselves aren't. The Protection T6 itemization loses a lot of strength and agility compared to previous tiers and, instead, focuses primarily on stamina and, unfortunately, shield block rating (on 4 out of 5 pieces), which is the least desirable stat as a Protection Warrior. As a result, the Protection T6 items are all one-dimensional, and with a poorly distributed item budget, which completely negates the value of the set bonuses.

On the other hand, the Fury version of the Tier 6 comes with excellent stats to increase our threat, notably the new addition of Armor Penetration, which is one of the rare stats that affects all our spells across the board.
However, there are two important caveats to the use of the Fury Tier 6. First, the lack of defense rating or resilience on so many items makes it difficult to maintain your critical strikes immunity. And second, we now share our token Helm of the Forgotten Protector Helm of the Forgotten Protector with two of the most represented classes in the TBC meta, which can account for up to half of the raid in some guilds and make Tier 6 items extremely contested.

For these reasons, Phases 3 and 4 are the phases where it is the most important that you take advantage of the free Season 3 Arena gear to form your BiS set. Several of the S3 armor pieces are BiS for their stats alone, but they also come with an enormous amount of resilience that makes reaching your critical strike immunity all the easier.
I can not insist enough on the benefits of taking advantage of this gear path that is not only free and guaranteed (one armor piece every 4-5 weeks by simply playing 10 games in a 5v5 team with your friends), but also reduces the competition on T6 tokens for the benefit of your entire guild.

Aldor vs. Scryer for Protection Warrior Tank in Phase 4
Choosing between The Aldor or The Scryers determines which shoulders inscription you can access upon reaching exalted, which are basically equal for either side. But more importantly, your choice will affect the proc of your Shattered Sun Offensive necklaces in Phase 5, which should have a greater influence on your decision.

The Inscriptions
Mathematically speaking, the Aldor's inscription Greater Inscription of Warding Greater Inscription of Warding totals slightly more raw avoidance at 1.30% avoidance, while the Scryers' Greater Inscription of the Knight Greater Inscription of the Knight provides 1.28% avoidance. This minuscule difference, at the scale of your entire gear set, is only a 0.06% difference in total mitigation value, which is to say negligible and irrelevant.
Math aside, it is worth mentioning that more defense can help facilitate your gearing process in the early phases. This can be a good argument for choosing the Scryers, as you replace a lot of gear fast and have to adjust your crit immunity constantly.

The Necklace
The Shattered Sun Offensive tank necklace, Shattered Sun Pendant of Resolve Shattered Sun Pendant of Resolve, available in Phase 5, and once you are exalted with this new faction, comes in two versions based on your chosen Shattrath faction. The Aldor version has a +100 dodge rating proc (+5.28% dodge) while the Scryers version has a +100 expertise rating proc (-6.25% dodge/parry).

Some napkin math:

    The proc chance is 15% on hit, with a 10 sec duration and 45 sec ICD. Assuming a generous 22% uptime, we can simplify it to an additional 22 dodge or expertise rating. In practice, the expertise proc will be more valuable since you will pair it with your cooldowns on pull for snap threat. The dodge proc will also be more valuable, since avoidance EP scales exponentially.
    Using Emrus' tank spreadsheet to determine Protection Warrior EP, that puts the Scryers version at 186 EP (90 Threat, 96 Mitigation) and the Aldor version at 152 EP (62 Threat, 90 Mitigation). For comparison, Collar of the Pit Lord Collar of the Pit Lord totals 196 EP (74 Threat, 122 Mitigation).
    When comparing these items, the threat value of expertise is halved because you are already soft-capped in Phase 3-4 gear. Moreover, you will be hard-capped in SWP BiS before the proc, making the Scryers version useless in the long run. On the other hand, Pit Lord will always be a better mitigation neck than the Aldor version.


In conclusion, whichever faction you pick does not matter much in the long run. The shoulder enchants are identical, and the necklace is just a stepping stone between Phase 4 and Phase 5 gear. Assuming your guild is capable of progressing through Sunwell Plateau and killing Brutallus, chances are you will have a better necklace before reaching Exalted with the Shattered Sun Offensive.
However, if you are a more casual player that may not have access to T6 or SWP gear, then I recommend siding with The Aldor for a great mitigation necklace, since you will be able to buy Brooch of Deftness Brooch of Deftness for a threat option with your Badge of Justice Badge of Justice in Phase 4.

If you need to grind reputation with The Aldor or The Scryers, or any of the other factions that offer BiS gear for Protection Warriors, take a look at our guides to learn the fastest way to reach the rank you'll need.

Best in Slot Head Armor for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 4
Despite its low threat value, Faceplate of the Impenetrable is an excellent tank helmet with very high mitigation stats. By virtue of being a drop from the final boss of the Black Temple, this item benefits from an inflated item budget, which you should definitely take advantage of. The one-dimensional stats of this helmet can be balanced with more offensive items in other slots, overall giving you more total stats from your gear set.

For a more offensive approach, Vengeful Gladiator's Plate Helm is an amazing helmet for threat value with a large amount of stam and resilience that will greatly facilitate maintaining your crit immunity this phase. The lack of defense or resilience on Helm of the Illidari Shatterer makes it more difficult to fit in your gear set.

Alternatively, Onslaught Greathelm and Onslaught Battle-Helm are good choices for mitigation and threat, respectively, but a poor usage of very contested Helm of the Forgotten Protector Helm of the Forgotten Protector tier tokens given the better, uncontested options. I recommend using proper fury gems and enchants on these DPS items so that they remain versatile and useful when not tanking as well.

Battleworn Tuskguard from Zul'Aman is completely barren of threat stats but a strong mitigation alternative nonetheless for niche applications, like soaking Saber Lash against Mother Shahraz.

    Why replace Powerful Earthstorm Diamond Powerful Earthstorm Diamond for Relentless Earthstorm Diamond Relentless Earthstorm Diamond this phase?
    It's a combination of factors. First off, tank items are very tanky this phase, with enormous amounts of stamina which lets us reach a comfortable survival level with fewer items and thus add more threat-oriented items to balance it out. This increase in AP, crit rating, and armor penetration compared to previous phases makes Impale more valuable starting from 18-20% crit chance before buffs. On top of that, deadlier bosses and faster weapons lead to a higher usage of Heroic Strike, which further increases the value of Impale. All of these factors synergize very well with the additional critical damage multiplier from Relentless Earthstorm Diamond Relentless Earthstorm Diamond, whose activation requirements can be used to finesse our hit cap.

In the meantime, the trusty Shapeshifter's Signet Shapeshifter's Signet stays on!

Band of the Eternal Champion is the second-best ring for threat, thanks primarily to the high Agility and Stamina, which also comes with a strong attack power proc every minute. Alternatively, Ring of Deceitful Intent Ring of Deceitful Intent is a very similar ring option with hit rating in place of the AP proc.

For mitigation rings, Band of the Eternal Defender immediately comes to mind, but there is a catch... The Armor proc does not stack with your usual Scroll of Protection V Scroll of Protection V and, even worse, removes your scroll buff entirely. Unfortunately, this low proc chance, <10% uptime armor buff, ends up only hurting your overall mitigation compared to the permanent effect of a Scroll of Protection V Scroll of Protection V, which makes the exalted tank ring simply unusable for any serious tank using scrolls. The only way to circumvent this undesirable proc is to never upgrade your ring past the revered version Band of Eternity with the same stats, minus the proc. This also means that warriors who hastily picked the DPS version of the ring first will never have access to the revered tank version. Silly design or unintended spaghetti code, we'll never know...

Band of the Abyssal Lord is a great mitigation-oriented ring with hit, which can come in handy to both fill a need for hit rating and defense. Violet Signet of the Great Protector and Ring of Sundered Souls are still the best mitigation-only rings, great for a Mother Shahraz's Saber Lash soaker set.

Best in Slot Trinkets for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 4
Trinkets are an ideal slot to adjust your threat or mitigation needs on the fly with a large variety of options ranging from pure threat to pure stamina. It is generally advised to avoid relying on trinkets to achieve your crit immunity to preserve this freedom of juggling different choices based on the encounter.

For mitigation, Phase 3 introduced the very coveted Darkmoon Card: Vengeance and Darkmoon Card: Madness decks. The former is especially sought after because of its proc, which provides an additional ~15 TPS. Stamina trinkets are always a great choice for a Protection Warrior because they provide excellent value in this slot and let you use more offensive items in other slots once your survival is assured.

If you can't afford Darkmoon Faire decks, your best options remain the various +45 Stam trinkets. Commander's Badge, Balebrew Charm, and Brightbrew Charm especially are pretty strong because of the various guardians they summon. While their damage doesn't contribute to your threat, they can still do some decent work during their 30-40 seconds of activation time.

Now let's look at threat options. Because your abilities don't scale very well with offensive stats overall, the best way to increase your threat with trinkets is to raise your hit and expertise rating to make sure you land as many of your attacks as possible. There is no expertise trinket available until phase 5, so in the meantime, Icon of Unyielding Courage or Romulo's Poison Vial are still the best threat trinkets available in phase 4, as long as you build your gear set around the huge amount of hit rating they provide. Icon provides a nice snap threat boost on pull when you land your first couple rotations of abilities under the effect of Armor Penetration, while Romulo's poison proc adds a more steady damage source equivalent to ~12 TPS.

If you don't need this amount of hit rating, however, and still want to use a threat-oriented trinket over a stamina one, I recommend setting your eyes on a snap threat trinket that will boost your threat output at the beginning of the fight when threat is the most volatile. Abacus of Violent Odds provides the most explosive threat increase and is easy enough to farm. Badge of the Swarmguard is a close second but less accessible.

After that, your remaining options are to compete with DPS for very coveted upgrades such as Dragonspine Trophy, Berserker's Call, Madness of the Betrayer or Tsunami Talisman, which provide good sustained threat but are less useful than snap threat trinkets. I simply can not recommend ever pursuing these items, considering the poor effectiveness on a tank instead of a DPS that would get triple the value out of every single one of these big-ticket trinkets.

Shadowmoon Insignia or Battlemaster's Cruelty are niche trinkets. Both provide mediocre mitigation or threat, respectively, with a gimmicky on-use effect Protector's Vigor increasing your HP by 1750 for 20 sec. Some people like them, some people hate them. Because their value comes from their activation at the appropriate moment, it's very difficult to gauge how useful they really are. Personally, I find the on-use very lackluster relative to the 20-21k HP pool of a buffed tank, especially against bosses swinging at you for ~6k damage, and redundant with the Protection Warrior toolkit. Last Stand and Nightmare Seed Nightmare Seed are much better HP buffer and panic buttons when you need them, and don't cost any gear slot.

Weapons and Offhands for Protection Warrior Tank in Phase 4
In Phase 3, the damage output of bosses ramped up significantly. While Morogrim Tidewalker was the only "tank buster" of Phase 2, you should expect to take a similar beating from half of the bosses in Phase 3. This increase in damage taken across the board can be used to our advantage, however, with faster weapons to convert more rage into Heroic Strike. For this reason, weapon speed becomes a major factor to generate threat on every rage-capped encounter in Phases 3 and 4, and, lucky for us, there are a lot of options to pick from.

The Brutalizer is the weapon goal for all Protection Warriors, except Human Humans. The Brutalizer has excellent stats all around, with both Expertise and high speed to increase your threat generation, and Stamina and Defense for a high mitigation value as well. Orc Orcs, in particular, will receive a massive threat upgrade from the synergy with their Axe Specialization, doubling the expertise gained from this item. Human tanks, however, will prefer to stick to swords and maces to take advantage of their own racials; Sword Specialization and Mace Specialization.

With a swing speed of 1.4 seconds, Blade of Savagery is the best weapon out there for threat in a rage-capped situation, for example, against any hard hitter such as Azalgor, Archimonde, Teron Gorefiend, or Illidan Stormrage.

Humans especially will gain a huge threat boost from this sword compared to previous phases, but The Brutalizer remains the recommended choice for other races due to the added mitigation value and less "glass-canon" weapon. However, Blade of Savagery is also a coveted weapon for DPS Warrior Warriors and Rogue Rogues. In the meantime, Swiftsteel Bludgeon is a good alternative with close threat value and no competition.

Zul'Aman noteworthy additions are Akil'zon's Talonblade, a fast threat-oriented sword comparable to Swiftsteel Bludgeon in threat output but without hit rating, and Cleaver of the Unforgiving Cleaver of the Unforgiving, a good axe alternative for Orc Orcs still waiting for The Brutalizer.

    Because Blade of Savagery or Swiftsteel Bludgeon have some hit rating, they will change your BiS combination for hit cap!
    6% hit balanced set example for Human with Blade of Savagery.

The Unbreakable Will is the best mitigation weapon available still in Phase 4, with Stamina, Defense, and bonus armor. Despite the lack of offensive stats, its high DPS and fast speed still make it a decent weapon for threat, especially for Humans.

    Mongoose or Executioner for a tank's weapon?
    Mongoose provides both threat, in the form of attack speed and crit, as well as some mitigation from the agility converted to dodge. Executioner is a slight TPS increase over Mongoose (~0.9%), but losing the agility increases your damage taken by ~3.4%. All in all, the TPS: DTPS trade-off of replacing Mongoose by Executioner isn't very interesting for us.

Burning Crusade Classic Protection Warrior Tank Best in Slot (BiS) Phase 5 Gear Guide
By Beanna
Updated: 2026/02/24
Welcome to Wowhead's Best in Slot Gear list from Phase 5 Sunwell Plateau for Protection Warrior Tank in The Burning Crusade Classic. This guide will list the recommended gear for Protection Warrior Tank to acquire while they progress during the fifth and final phase of The Burning Crusade Classic, which contains gear sourced from Sunwell Plateau, Zul'Aman, Black Temple, and Mount Hyjal, plus other pieces from previous phases like Serpentshrine Cavern, Tempest Keep, heroic dungeons, arena PvP Gear, professions, BoE World Drops, and reputations.
Best In Slot Gear for Protection Warrior Tank in Phase 5
Gearing your Protection Warrior in TBCC highly depends on your raiding environment. Some guilds may require a higher threat ceiling to withstand the pressure from high-performing damage dealers, while other guilds may encourage erring on the side of caution and focusing solely on survival. Most guilds will require a little bit of both, and it is up to each tank to decide on the balance between threat and mitigation they want to achieve based on their experience within their group and for different encounters.

For this reason, I have made a selection of the most balanced and budget-efficient items for each slot as well as a threat and a mitigation-oriented alternative when applicable.

It is also important to note that gearing as a Protection Warrior in TBCC requires constant adjustments to maintain your crit immunity as you upgrade your gear, and you may lose Defense or Resilience rating in some slots. Make sure you keep some item alternatives to mix and match your stats and meet your goals for survival and threat.

The Protection Warrior Tier 6 set Onslaught Armor comes with two good set bonuses.

    2 pieces: Improved Commanding Shout Increases the health bonus from your Commanding Shout ability by 170.
    4 pieces: Improved Shield Slam Increases the damage of your Shield Slam ability by 10%.

The original five pieces of the Protection Tier 6, available starting from phase 3, are poorly itemized overall, making otherwise valuable set bonuses not worth pursuing. However, Sunwell Plateau introduces three new items to the T6 set — the bracers, the belt and the boots — which are all the best item for their respective slot in phase 5, meaning you now only need one more tier piece to unlock the Improved Shield Slam set bonus. The chest or the shoulders are the recommended go-to 4th piece in a balanced set since they are the smallest stat loss compared to SWP options.

Sunwell Plateau is a big step up in difficulty and incoming damage for a tank compared to the watered-down phase 3 raids, because of greatly increased boss damage paired with Sunwell Radiance nerfing tanks' avoidance significantly. Lady Sacrolash and Entropius are the "tame bosses" of SWP, and they already hit as hard as Illidan Stormrage and Archimonde, respectively. Felmyst is about 1.5x Illidan with a lot of magic damage, and Brutallus is closer to a permanently enraged Illidan.
Because of this daunting damage output, picking high survivability gear is essential, and a purely mitigation-focused gear set will see a lot more usage than in previous phases. A balanced gear set with threat in mind is still useful, nonetheless, against less threatening bosses or to revisit previous raids.

    What is the mysterious Sunwell Radiance?
    Sunwell Radiance is an invisible buff that affects every enemy creatures and bosses inside Sunwell Plateau up to the Eredar Twins included (so not active on M'uru or Kil'jaeden), that increases their chances to hit by +5% and reduces the chances to dodge of surrounding players by -20%, for a total of -25% avoidance for players. Although Blizzard never officially communicated their reasons to introduce this buff, the most likely theory is that they realized that tanks were on a trajectory to reach complete immunity to melee damage due to the out-of-hand powercreep of item levels. To remedy the situation, they chose to nerf players inside Sunwell Plateau specifically with this bandaid buff, instead of overhauling ratings and stats conversions that would negatively affect all players across the board.

    Bonus: Phase 3-4 Mitigation BiS for Brutallus progression

Protection Warrior Gems & Enchants

Aldor vs. Scryer for Protection Warrior Tank in Phase 5
Choosing between The Aldor or The Scryers determines which shoulders inscription you can access upon reaching exalted, which are basically equal for either side. But more importantly, your choice will affect the proc of your Shattered Sun Offensive necklaces in Phase 5, which should have a greater influence on your decision.

The Inscriptions
Mathematically speaking, the Aldor's inscription Greater Inscription of Warding Greater Inscription of Warding totals slightly more raw avoidance at 1.30% avoidance, while the Scryers' Greater Inscription of the Knight Greater Inscription of the Knight provides 1.28% avoidance. This minuscule difference, at the scale of your entire gear set, is only a 0.06% difference in total mitigation value, which is to say negligible and irrelevant.
Math aside, it is worth mentioning that more defense can help facilitate your gearing process in the early phases. This can be a good argument for choosing the Scryers, as you replace a lot of gear fast and have to adjust your crit immunity constantly.

The Necklace
The Shattered Sun Offensive tank necklace, Shattered Sun Pendant of Resolve Shattered Sun Pendant of Resolve, available in Phase 5, and once you are exalted with this new faction, comes in two versions based on your chosen Shattrath faction. The Aldor version has a +100 dodge rating proc (+5.28% dodge) while the Scryers version has a +100 expertise rating proc (-6.25% dodge/parry).

Some napkin math:

    The proc chance is 15% on hit, with a 10 sec duration and 45 sec ICD. Assuming a generous 22% uptime, we can simplify it to an additional 22 dodge or expertise rating. In practice, the expertise proc will be more valuable since you will pair it with your cooldowns on pull for snap threat. The dodge proc will also be more valuable, since avoidance EP scales exponentially.
    Using Emrus' tank spreadsheet to determine Protection Warrior EP, that puts the Scryers version at 186 EP (90 Threat, 96 Mitigation) and the Aldor version at 152 EP (62 Threat, 90 Mitigation). For comparison, Collar of the Pit Lord Collar of the Pit Lord totals 196 EP (74 Threat, 122 Mitigation).
    When comparing these items, the threat value of expertise is halved because you are already soft-capped in Phase 3-4 gear. Moreover, you will be hard-capped in SWP BiS before the proc, making the Scryers version useless in the long run. On the other hand, Pit Lord will always be a better mitigation neck than the Aldor version.


In conclusion, whichever faction you pick does not matter much in the long run. The shoulder enchants are identical, and the necklace is just a stepping stone between Phase 4 and Phase 5 gear. Assuming your guild is capable of progressing through Sunwell Plateau and killing Brutallus, chances are you will have a better necklace before reaching Exalted with the Shattered Sun Offensive.
However, if you are a more casual player that may not have access to T6 or SWP gear, then I recommend siding with The Aldor for a great mitigation necklace, since you will be able to buy Brooch of Deftness Brooch of Deftness for a threat option with your Badge of Justice Badge of Justice in Phase 4.

If you need to grind reputation with The Aldor or The Scryers, or any of the other factions that offer BiS gear for Protection Warriors, take a look at our guides to learn the fastest way to reach the rank you'll need.

Best in Slot Trinkets for Protection Warrior in TBC Classic Phase 5
Trinkets are an ideal slot to adjust your threat or mitigation needs on the fly with a large variety of options ranging from pure threat to pure stamina. It is generally advised to avoid relying on trinkets to achieve your crit immunity to preserve this freedom of juggling different choices based on the encounter.

After four phases of dreaming of trinkets with expertise — because frankly that's the only substantial scaling we have — they are finally here! And in the end, none of them is actually BiS because all that Sunwell Plateau gear is loaded with expertise, so much that we reach the 14% parry cap without even trying, and can't fit any of these hyped trinkets in a complete gear set. Oh, cruel irony.

Nonetheless, Shard of Contempt and Steely Naaru Sliver are still amazing threat trinkets to use before you inevitably collect enough gear to make all that expertise useless. Both are quite excellent for a Protection Warrior, with roughly 50% more threat value than the forbidden fruitsDragonspine Trophy, Madness of the Betrayer, or Tsunami Talisman that were so mediocre on a tank all along. Shard of Contempt is more "equip and forget", since everything about it is passive, while Steely Naaru Sliver has that "mini Last Stand" on-use that you may prefer depending on your tank habits.

Once you reach that 56 Expertise hard cap (14%) and can no longer benefit from the aforementioned trinkets, it's back to previous phases picks: Icon of Unyielding Courage or Romulo's Poison Vial are still excellent, as long as you can make use of the hit. And if not, then Abacus of Violent Odds provides the most explosive threat increase and is easy enough to farm. Badge of the Swarmguard is a close second, but less accessible.

Mitigation trinkets in this phase can be separated into two categories. There are your general mitigation trinkets for every purpose, and then there are your specific on-use trinkets to mitigate Brutallus's deadly Stomp.

For general mitigation, the only new addition to your collection in Phase 5 is the amazing Commendation of Kael'thas. It comes with more stamina than any other trinket in the game, as well as a neat dodge bonus that triggers anytime your HP drops below 35%, like an automatic panic cooldown, albeit minor (+8% dodge).

After that, your best friends remain the trusty Darkmoon Card: Vengeance and Darkmoon Card: Madness decks, with a small preference for Vengeance because the proc provides an additional passive ~15 TPS.

If you are on a budget, the next best options remain the various +45 Stam trinkets. Commander's Badge, Balebrew Charm and Brightbrew Charm especially are quite interesting because of the various guardians they summon. While their damage doesn't contribute to your threat, they can still do some decent work during their 30-40 seconds of activation time, or be used as cannon fodder to soak cleave mechanics, for example.

Now for general mitigation specifically, apart from Commendation of Kael'thas that should always be your first choice for mitigation, you may want to prefer an on-use trinket to beef up your survival when it matters the most: to increase your chances of surviving the 10 seconds Stomp debuff that reduces your armor by 50%, leading to a ~40% increase in damage taken during that debuff. Thankfully, the Protection Warrior is loaded with tools to mitigate the danger of this debuff, with a rotation of Last Stand, Nightmare Seed Nightmare Seed, or Shield Wall, but nonetheless, some active trinkets can rise in value during these critical seconds.

The Ancient Aqir Artifact fills this niche perfectly, because its on-use bonus armor is not affected by the 50% reduction from Stomp. It excels at reinforcing your mitigation when you need it the most, and its passive value (+1.90% parry) throughout the entire fight remains decent.
Good alternatives are Shadowmoon Insignia, with the highest passive value but a slightly longer cooldown, or the good old Moroes' Lucky Pocket Watch catching dust in your bank since Phase 1, which has a peak value that lasts exactly the duration of a full Stomp.

Weapons and Offhands for Protection Warrior Tank in Phase 5
Since phase 3, the damage output of raid bosses has ramped up significantly, making rage generation when tanking an aftersouth and weapon speed a priority to convert more rage into Heroic Strike and thus more threat.

Phase 5 only introduces one tank upgrade to your Blade of Savagery, The Brutalizer, or Swiftsteel Bludgeon, and that weapon is the Dragonscale-Encrusted Longblade. Contrary to previous phases, this time there are no different BiS weapons based on your race choice and weapon specialization traits. All of this is irrelevant when any race can reach expertise cap with any weapon type, thanks to an overkill amount of expertise rating available on all Sunwell Plateau gear.

Because of its slightly slower speed, Blade of Savagery users won't notice a real threat increase when upgrading to the Dragonscale-Encrusted Longblade, but the better mitigation stats are the cherry on top. On the other hand, tanks upgrading from The Brutalizer will see a slight drop in survival from losing some defense rating but gain double the difference in threat, making it a very beneficial trade-off. Plus, it sheathes in the back, and you can't beat those style points.

    Because Human Human will have an extra 5 expertise when wielding Dragonscale-Encrusted Longblade, their BiS gear set is slightly different to not overcap expertise.
    6% hit balanced set example for Human Human.

Fang of Kalecgos deserves a mention as a catch-up weapon for tanks still waiting on a Black Temple drop. That dagger is disenchant-status from the start anyway because no class or spec uses it.

The Unbreakable Will remains the best mitigation weapon still in phase 5, with high Stamina, Defense, and bonus Armor, if you are aiming for the maximum mitigation gear set for Brutallus.

    Mongoose or Executioner for a tank's weapon?
    Mongoose provides both threat, in the form of attack speed and crit, as well as some mitigation from the agility converted to dodge. Executioner is a slight TPS increase over Mongoose (~0.9%), but losing the agility increases your damage taken by ~3.4%. All in all, the TPS: DTPS trade-off of replacing Mongoose by Executioner isn't very interesting for us.
